Finance covers what a student is charged, what has been paid, and the receipt that proves it. It is one page with four tabs, Collections, Payments, Student accounts and Fee structure (each shown only to those allowed to open it), and + Record payment always at the top right. Recording a payment, adding or editing a fee item, a payment and a student's account all open in a pop-up over the page you are on; Cancel, the × or a click outside closes it.

Collections: what came in, and where the fees stand

Collections opens with today's takings, this month's, and the total for the period you choose (7 days, 30 days, 90 days or 12 months) against the period before it. The chart shows every day, week or month in that period; hover over or tab to a column for its amount and number of payments, and Table under it lists the same figures. Beside it: how the money was paid (cash, bank transfer, POS, online) and, for a bursar or the School Admin, which member of staff took it.

For those who may see school-wide figures, Where the fees stand follows, for the session you pick: the share of charged fees collected so far (the striped part of the bar is owed and already past its due date), how many students have paid in full, part paid, paid nothing or have no fees yet (each a link to that list), the largest balances, and the same figures by class and by fee. A cashier sees only the money they recorded themselves, and no school-wide balance.

Student accounts

Student accounts lists every student enrolled in a session, or charged for it, with what they were charged, what has been applied to those charges, how far along they are, their balance (and how much of it is overdue) and the date of their last payment. Narrow it by class with the chips, by where students stand (Owing, Nothing paid, Part paid, Paid in full, No fees yet), or by name or admission number, and order it by largest balance, name, class, most paid or lowest share paid. Each choice loads in place. The figures across the top always add up the students shown.

Fee items

Fee Structure is where a school defines what it charges — tuition, a term's dues, a one-off charge — and which classes each applies to (+ Add fee item), charges students, and looks back over every charge made. This is the fourth step of the new-school setup checklist.

Billing a whole class

On Fee Structure, the top of the page chooses between Bill a student, for one child, and Bill a class, for everyone enrolled in a class for the session. Pick the session, the class, the billing period and, if you want one, a due date; the fees offered are the ones assigned to that class.

  • Review first. Review shows how many students will be billed, each fee and the total per student. Nothing is charged until you confirm on that screen.
  • The progress window stays open. The class is billed in batches. The window closes only once every student on the list is confirmed as billed. If a student could not be billed, the window lists who and why; Retry the students not yet billed bills only those.
  • Safe to run again. A student already charged a fee for that billing period is skipped, so billing the class again after an error never charges anyone twice.
  • Archiving. Archiving or activating a fee asks first. Archiving stops it being offered for new charges; charges and payments already made are not changed.

Recording a payment

+ Record payment opens a short form in a pop-up: who paid (type part of the student's name or admission number to narrow the list; it shows where the receipt will be sent) and what was paid (the amount, what it is for, how it was paid, and a reference or note if there is one). Saving makes a numbered receipt at once, sends it to the guardian, and opens the payment's own page, ready to apply it to fees.

Payments lists every payment: all of them, those not yet applied to a fee, those fully applied, or voided ones, for one session or every session, by method, or found by student, admission number, receipt number or payer. A cashier sees only the payments they recorded.

A payment's own page

Opening a payment (from Payments, Collections or a student's account) shows everything about it in one place: the receipt exactly as issued, with Print receipt and Open PDF; Apply to fees; Send to the family (email the receipt, or send the payment text, again); and, for those who manage finance, Correct a mistake. A payment can be voided there, with a reason, if it was entered wrong; voiding never deletes it, it just stops it counting. Voiding is only ever a correction to the record - for a payment a parent actually made online, see Refunding an online payment below, which is the action that sends the money itself back.

Deciding what a payment covers

A payment on its own is just an amount received — applying it is what says which particular charge (this term's tuition, say, or the uniform levy) it pays off. On the payment's page, Apply to fees lists the student's fees still owing for that session, each with how it is billed and what it still owes. Type an amount against each, use Fill to give one fee as much as it owes, or Fill oldest first to pay the oldest fees until the money runs out; the running total shows what is left, and Apply saves it. A payment can cover several charges at once, or only part of one, and what is left still shows as not yet applied.

For as long as any posted payment - by any means, including one paid online - has not been allocated to a charge, every finance officer sees a banner naming how many and how much, on every page, not just Finance. It is not a message you can dismiss: it goes away on its own, the moment the last one is allocated, precisely so a payment can never be quietly forgotten.

A student's own fee account

A student's account (opened from Student accounts, from their own page under Students, or from a payment's receipt) opens in a pop-up and shows, for the session you choose: every fee charged and how it is billed (One-time, Full Session, or First, Second or Third Term), what has been paid against each and what is still owed; and every payment made in that session, with the fees it paid and any part not yet applied. Receipt opens that payment in the same pop-up, and + Record a payment starts one for this student — the same figures a linked parent sees in their own version.

The receipt

Every school's receipt carries its own branding — logo, colours, name, address, phone, a signature and, if set, the bursar's own notes — drawn from the same description on screen, in print and in the PDF a parent is sent, so all three always agree. A receipt can be re-sent by email (and the payment text by SMS), or downloaded again, at any time. Receipt settings is where that authorised signature image is uploaded, and where a school can choose its own receipt-number prefix, once, for the whole school.

Recording a payment automatically tells the parent — an in-app alert, an email with the receipt attached and a text message — the moment it is saved.

What a parent sees

A linked parent sees their child's full fee account: what has been charged, what has been paid, and the outstanding balance, with every receipt available to download again.

Letting a parent pay online

Online Payments connects your school's own Paystack account — enter the public and secret keys from your Paystack dashboard, and save. It is School Admin only, even for a Finance Manager, since it decides where the school's money goes. Once set up, every parent sees a "Pay online" box on their child's fee account, for any amount up to what is outstanding; they pay by card, bank transfer or however Paystack itself offers, and the payment appears in your records — with its own receipt and parent notification — automatically, the moment Paystack confirms it, exactly as if a member of staff had typed it in. There is no shared platform account behind this: until a school sets up its own, parents simply do not see the option.

Paste the webhook address shown on this page into Paystack's own dashboard. It is what lets a payment still be recorded correctly even if the parent closes their browser the moment they finish paying, before being sent back to the portal. Even without that, the system still checks on its own the next time the parent or a finance officer opens a relevant page, so a payment Paystack actually took is never simply lost to a missed webhook.

Refunding an online payment

A payment a parent made online has its own Refund via Paystack button on the receipt page, alongside Void - the same finance permission, but a different action: this one asks Paystack itself to send the money back, rather than just correcting the record. Give a reason and confirm; the payment is not marked refunded straight away, because starting a refund is not the same as Paystack having actually sent the money back yet. Once Paystack confirms it, the payment is voided automatically, with a note that it was refunded, not just corrected by hand - and if Paystack instead reports the refund failed, the payment is left exactly as it was, so nothing is ever marked refunded that did not really happen. A payment recorded by hand, or already refunded, does not offer this button - void it instead, and settle the parent outside the system, the same way as always.
