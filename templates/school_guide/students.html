A student account is never self-registered — staff always create it, one at a time or many at once — and it always starts on a one-time password the student must replace at first sign-in.

The students list

Students opens on every class at a glance: how many students each has, boys and girls, and how many can sign in to the student portal, with a count of anyone not placed in a class this session. Choose a class with the chips at the top (or a row of the table) and its register loads in place; the search box finds a student in that class, or in any class when none is chosen.

{{ shot('students', 'Students: every class with its numbers. Choosing a class lists its students in place.') }}

In a class split into subdivisions (JSS 1A, JSS 1B), each student's subdivision is shown under their name, and the class teacher of a subdivision sees only its students here.

Clicking a student opens them in a pop-up over the register: their class (and subdivision), department and admission number at the top, four figures (term results, overall mark, assignments, projects), and one tab at a time for their Profile (personal details, parent or guardian, and their portal login), Term results, Every mark, Assignments, Projects and School journey. Edit record, Fee account, a new password, and Correct on a journey entry open in a second pop-up on top; saving there brings you back to the student, up to date. Close a pop-up with ×, Escape or a click outside it.

Registering one student

+ Register a student opens in a pop-up and creates the student record, enrols them in the class and session you choose, and generates their admission number and login in one step. The admission number is always generated from your school's own numbering rules — nobody types one in by hand, so it can never clash with another student's.

For a student in SSS 1 to 3, the form also asks for their department (Science, Art or Commercial). It can be left as Not chosen yet and filled in later; until then the student is treated as taking every subject of their class, so nothing is hidden from them.

If the class is split into subdivisions, the student is placed in one automatically, in turn, into the one with the fewest students (in SSS, one of their department's or one with no department). The same happens to students imported in bulk and to candidates admitted from the entrance exam. To move a student later, their class teacher uses Class transfers; see {{ page('classes-subjects', 'Classes and subjects') }}.

Bringing in new students at once

Use Bulk import from the Students page to bring students in from a spreadsheet. The page has three steps, shown one at a time, and they are done in order: students, then their enrolment history, then their past results. Each step has its own template to download and its own rules. Upload the filled-in sheet as a CSV or straight from Excel as an .xlsx workbook: only its first sheet is read, and formulas are never run (the value Excel saved is what is imported). A workbook with macros (.xlsm), or one protected by a password, is refused; save it as a plain .xlsx or as CSV.

Step 1 creates each student exactly as registering one by hand does. Every column is required, and every row must fill them all in: first_name, middle_name, last_name, gender, class, guardian_name, guardian_email and guardian_phone. The guardian's details drive the alerts parents receive and their sign-in, so a row without them is skipped. A row that is not valid is skipped and reported by its line number; every other row is still imported. Admission numbers are generated, never typed.

The generated usernames and one-time passwords for an import are shown once, on the results page, and can be downloaded from there as a CSV. They are never stored anywhere in readable form afterwards, so copy or download them before you navigate away.

A third way in: the entrance exam

A student can also arrive through the {{ page('admissions', 'admissions waitlist') }}, in the Entrance workspace: once a candidate has sat their entrance exam, an admissions officer can admit them straight into a real student record, the same way as the other two paths above.

Enrolment history: each student's past years

Step 2 brings in a student's past classes, one row per student per year, matched by admission number. Required columns: admission_no, level, session. Optional: enrolled_at, completed_at, outcome, notes.

  • Dates must be written YYYY-MM-DD, for example 2023-09-11, in both enrolled_at and completed_at. Leave completed_at empty while the year is open.
  • outcome is promoted, repeated, graduated or withdrawn. A graduated row archives the student once that year is recorded.
  • A session is matched by its name, such as 2023/2024. It may be a session the school has since archived.
  • If the session does not exist and it is an earlier year than the current session, the import creates it. The new session is inactive and is never made the current session, and the import records who created it and why. An import never changes which session is current.
  • A session that is later than the current one, or that is not written as 2023/2024, is refused. Create those by hand under Academic sessions.

Creating an earlier session, and archiving a graduate, need the permissions to import students and to deactivate students. The bulk-import permission covers both (see Roles, permissions and scope).

A student's account afterwards

From a student's pop-up you can edit their details, reset their password to a new one-time password (shown once, with a button to print it), or turn their sign-in off or on without touching anything else about their record. Enrolment history — previous schools, earlier levels — is kept separately and can be corrected with a reason if it was entered wrong.

ID cards

ID cards (in the menu under Management, Design & print) prints student and staff ID cards from the school's own design, in its colours. It needs the right to print ID cards (the Records Officer, Head Teacher and Class Teacher roles have it). A class teacher sees and prints only their own class's students (or, for the class teacher of a subdivision, that subdivision's), never staff cards; a subject teacher has no ID cards. Designing the cards is the ID card studio's (see {{ page('design-studios', 'Design studios') }}).

  • Printing. Choose Students (by class) or Staff, tick the people and press Print ticked cards. A sheet opens with ten bank-card-size cards to an A4 page and cut marks; with Print the backs too, each page of fronts is followed by its backs, arranged for double-sided printing (flip on the long edge). Print at 100%, on card or on paper to laminate. The first print issues each person a card with its own number (STU-000001, STF-000001) and QR code; printing again reuses it. A student without a photograph is marked, so you can add one on their page first.
  • The ID card studio. Design the student card (or staff card) opens the studio, where the card is designed freely, front and back, at its real proportions:
    • Details puts any of the person's details on the card with one click. For a student: name, middle name, admission number, class, department, session, gender, date of birth, age, blood group, genotype, state of origin, religion, and the parent's name and phone. For staff: title, name, role, job title, department, teaching or non-teaching, full-time or part-time, date employed, staff number, gender, date of birth, state of origin, phone, email and an emergency contact (the next of kin's name and phone, from Staff records). And the school's name, motto, address, phone and email, the card number and the date issued. A printed card carries only the details its design shows.
    • Insert text, a detail, the photograph, the logo, the QR code, a barcode, the head's signature and, on staff cards, the card holder's own signature; and shapes: rectangles, ellipses, triangles, diamonds, hexagons, stars, chevrons, arrows, waves, corner sweeps and lines.
    • The school's images: upload a pattern, a badge or the school's stamp and place it anywhere, or use it as the card's background.
    • Choose an item to style it on the right: the font, size, weight, italics, capitals, spacing and alignment of text, with a coloured box behind it if wanted; the fill (any colour, or a gradient), outline and corners of a shape; the fit, frame and clip of a picture (a circle, a rounded box, a hexagon, a shield, an arch...); its turn, opacity and shadow. Colours can be the school's own, any colour picked, or typed as a code.
    • Drag to move (items snap to the card's edges and centre and to each other), drag the corners to resize (Shift keeps the shape), drag the round handle to turn. Drag a box round several, or Shift-click, to move them together or line them up and space them evenly. Layers sets what is in front, hides an item or locks it in place.
    • Undo and redo (Ctrl+Z, Ctrl+Y), copy, paste and duplicate (Ctrl+C, Ctrl+V, Ctrl+D), nudge with the arrow keys, zoom (Ctrl and the mouse wheel), and a grid.
    • Templates shows ready designs (Classic, Modern upright, Minimal, Bold) with your school's colours; click one to put it on the card and build on it. Undo brings your own design back, and nothing is kept until you save. Versions keeps every save; Bring back puts an earlier one on screen to save again.
    What is designed is exactly what prints: the print sheet draws every card the same way.
  • Lock. Lock saves the design and stops anyone changing it until someone allowed to manage ID cards unlocks it. An image a locked design uses cannot be removed. Every save, lock, print and upload is in the activity log.
  • The QR code. Scanning it opens a check page on the school's portal, with no sign-in: the school, the person's name, photograph and class or role, and whether the card is Valid. Nothing else about the person is shown.
  • A lost card. Replace cancels it (its QR code then says it is no longer valid) and issues a new card to print. Cancel cancels it without a new one. A student who leaves the school shows as not valid automatically.

Designing the card is explained, with pictures, in {{ page('design-studios', 'Design studios') }}.

Troubleshooting: ID cards

Printed cards are the wrong size.
Print at 100% ("Actual size" in the print window), never "Fit to page". Each card should measure 85.6 × 54 mm.
The backs do not line up with the fronts.
Print double-sided and flip on the long edge. If your printer still shifts them, print fronts and backs separately and feed the sheet back in.
A student's photograph is missing.
They have no photograph yet: add one on their page (the list marks students without one), then print again. The card shows their initials meanwhile.
A detail (date of birth, genotype, guardian) is blank on the card.
It has not been entered on the student's or staff member's record. Fill it in, then print the card again.
Scanning the QR code says the card is not valid.
The card was cancelled or replaced (print the new one), or the student has left the school.
"Some of those people are not ones you can print cards for".
One of the people ticked is outside your classes, or has left. Untick them; nothing is printed until every one is someone you may print for.
The design will not save.
It is locked: someone allowed to manage ID cards must unlock it first.

Archiving a student who has left

When a student leaves, archive them rather than deleting them. Archiving keeps their whole record and takes them out of everything current:

  • they leave the class register and the student count;
  • their sign-in stops working straight away, including a session that is already open;
  • their results, report cards and fee history stay on file.

To archive one student, open them and use Archive this student at the foot of their Profile. To archive several, tick them in the class register; a bar appears with a box for the reason and Archive selected. You can give a reason, such as "Left the school" or "Transferred out". Archiving needs the permission to deactivate students, and it applies only to students in your own classes.

A graduate is archived automatically when a history import records their graduating year (see step 2 above).

Archived students are kept in Archived students, which only a School Admin can open. Each archived student opens in the same pop-up as everyone else, marked as archived, with their results and school journey. A School Admin can restore one from that pop-up, or tick several in the list and use Restore selected; it puts them back in their class register and turns their sign-in back on.

Parents keep access to an archived child. The child's record, results and fee account stay on the parent's portal, and only the child's own sign-in stops.
Deactivating a student hides them without marking them as having left. The deactivate switch does not work on an archived student: restore them first.
