Running the school itself — its staff, its roles, its look, and who did what.

The top bar

Along the top of every page: Guide and Messages (with a count of unread messages), the notifications bell, and your own name and photo. Click your name to open a small menu with Sign out. The name at the top is where to look to sign out; the side menu no longer has it.

The side menu

The menu opens while you hover over it. A small box on its right edge, at the bottom of the screen, keeps it open: click it once and the menu stays open on every page until you click it again. While the menu is open from hovering, that box steps aside so it never covers an item.

The menu is in two parts. Teaching is the classroom's daily work: Classroom (classes, subjects, attendance) and Assessment (academics, marking, the exam timetable, report cards). Management is running the school: Students & parents (students, parents, class transfers, promotion), Staff (staff records, teachers' duties, staff attendance, staff leave, payroll), Finance, Design & print (the design studios and printing ID cards) and Store & library. Above them are Overview, Messages and My work (your own clock-in, payslips and leave); Settings and Switch workspace are at the foot.

You only see what your role allows. The two headings, Teaching and Management, appear only for someone who reaches both; a teacher sees just their own sections. Each entry has its own icon, so the closed menu can be read at a glance: a compass for Overview, two speech bubbles for Messages, rows of desks for Classes, a register with ticks for Attendance, an A+ for Academics, a document with a rosette for Report cards, a mortarboard for Students, a palette for Design studios, coins for Collections, books for the Library, a gear for Settings.

I cannot find something in the menu.
Look under its part: classes, attendance, academics and report cards are under Teaching; students, staff, money, the design studios, ID cards, the store and the library under Management; settings at the foot. If it is not there at all, your role does not include it: ask the School Admin.
The menu closes when I move away.
Click the small box at its bottom right edge to keep it open on every page.

Settings

Settings, at the foot of the menu, holds everything that sets the portal up, decides who can use it and records what happened: the school profile, academic sessions, class subdivisions, staff and their roles, report card settings, Email & SMS, message wording, online payments, receipts, controls and alerts, the activity log and who is online. You see only what your role allows.

{{ shot('settings', 'Settings opens on the list of every setting, by group, with what needs attention and the state of each setting beside it.') }}
  • The list of every setting, by group, is on the left of every Settings page, the first one included; a dot marks a setting that needs a look. Click one to open it beside the list, without leaving Settings.
  • Beside it, when Settings opens, a strip names anything that needs attention (online payments not connected, the head's signature missing, alerts left open), then At a glance gives each setting's state in one column, such as which session is current or whether Email & SMS is the school's own account.
  • Find a setting, at the top of the list, narrows it as you type; press / anywhere in Settings to jump to it, Enter to open the first match and Escape to clear it.
  • Inside a setting, the page opens with where you are (Settings / its group), its name, its state and its main action, such as + Add staff on Staff or + New role on Staff roles. All settings, at the top of the list, goes back to the start.
  • On a phone, Settings opens on the list itself. Inside a setting, the list folds into one button at the top naming the setting you are in; tap it to open the list from the bottom of the screen and choose another.
  • The entrance examination's set-up is a workspace of its own, so it opens as its own page (its entry carries a small arrow).
Four areas are kept for the School Admin alone — the account created when the school itself was set up, never a role you build or grant: Staff and Staff roles, Email & SMS, Online payments and the School profile. Nobody else can be given access to them, no matter what permissions their role carries; if you cannot see them in Settings, that is why, and it is normal for every account except the School Admin's own.

Staff

Teachers (in the menu under Management, Staff) shows who teaches what. The figures across the top count the gaps: classes and subdivisions with no class teacher, and subjects (in SSS, departments of a subject) taught by nobody. It has two tabs:

  • Class teachers: every class, with its subdivisions under it (in SSS, each department's subdivision). Beside each class teacher, Replace opens a pop-up where you choose who takes over at once, or Nobody to remove them; a class or subdivision without one has + Assign a class teacher.
  • Subject teachers: one line per teacher with the subjects they teach. The ⇄ button on a subject removes the teacher from it or hands it straight to someone else. + Subject gives that teacher one more subject from those nobody teaches yet, so a teacher can take on more subjects, or fewer, at any time. All duties opens every duty of that teacher at once. At the top of the tab, Not taught yet lists each subject without a teacher, with Assign.

Each subject in a class has one teacher (in SSS, one per department). On the staff form and on this page, a subject someone already teaches in that class is not offered: replace its teacher here instead. Every change is in the activity log.

{{ shot('teachers', 'Teachers: Class teachers by class and subdivision, and Subject teachers, each with Replace or remove.') }}

Staff, in Settings, is everyone who signs in to the school's side of the portal: teachers, the bursar, the secretary, the librarian. Each person has a card with their name, username, whether they are active or suspended, their roles, their contact details and when they last signed in, and it says in plain words what they can reach: full access (the School Admin), a teacher's own classes and subjects, a limited area, or standard access from their roles.

{{ shot('staff', 'Staff, in Settings. The figures at the top are also the filters; + Add staff is at the top right of the page.') }} {{ shot('staff-modal', 'Manage opens the person in a pop-up with four tabs: Profile, Roles, Teaching duties and Limits.') }}
  • The figures at the top (Everyone, Can sign in, Suspended) are also the filters, and the search finds anyone by name, username or email.
  • + Add staff and Manage open the person in a pop-up over the list.
  • The ⋯ menu on a card sends them a message, resets their password (they get a new one-time password) or suspends or reactivates their access. Suspending stops their sign-in straight away; their record stays, and they can be reactivated at any time. You cannot suspend or reset your own account.

The staff pop-up has four tabs, all one form, so moving between them keeps what you typed:

  • Profile: their name, username, contact details and photo. The username is how they sign in and cannot be changed once made.
  • Roles: tick every job they do, grouped from the most senior down (head teacher, class teacher, subject teacher), with a summary that updates as you tick. See beside a role opens that role in a second pop-up on top, to check or change what it allows; closing it brings you back to the staff member exactly as you left them.
  • Teaching duties, for teachers: the class they are class teacher of (or, in a class split into subdivisions, the subdivision, such as JSS 1A), and the subjects they teach in each class (and, in SSS, which department). Choose the class first: the subject list then shows only the subjects that class takes (set on {{ page('classes-subjects', 'Subjects') }}). See {{ page('roles-permissions', 'Roles, permissions and scope') }}.
  • Limits, rarely needed: keeps anyone else to chosen classes, subjects, sessions or question banks.
  • Overrides (the School Admin only) gives single permissions for the exceptional case.

The bar at the foot saves, cancels, resets their password or suspends them. When you add someone, or reset a password, their username and one-time password show in the same pop-up, ready to print; it is shown once, and must be changed at their first sign-in. Nobody, not even the School Admin, can see a password, only reset it.

Staff records and leave

Staff records (in the menu under Management, Staff) is the school's file on everyone it employs: teachers, and also the driver, the cleaners and the security men, who have no portal sign-in. It needs the right to view staff records; changing them needs the right to manage them. The HR Officer role has both, and may approve leave.

  • Adding people. Add a member of staff opens the form: the person, the job (staff number, job title, department, teaching or not, full-time or not, when they were employed and confirmed), their next of kin, and private HR notes. Link a record to the person's portal account, if they have one, so they can ask for leave themselves. Add records for them makes a record at once for every staff account without one, copying their name, phone, email and role; fill in the rest afterwards.
  • A record has four tabs: Profile; Qualifications and documents (a CV, a certificate, an appointment letter: a PDF, Word document, JPG or PNG, opened only by people who may see staff records); Career (appointments, confirmation, promotions, changes of department, commendations, queries, warnings and suspensions; a promotion with a new job title changes it on the record); and Leave.
  • Nothing quietly disappears. A career entry cannot be changed or deleted. One made by mistake is struck out with the reason: it stays, crossed through. Opening a record or a document, and every change, is in the activity log, which names what changed but never the personal details themselves.
  • Your own record. Nobody in HR changes their own record, allowance, career or leave: someone else in HR does (the School Admin, who has nobody above them, may change their own). Each refusal is in the activity log.
  • Leaving. Set the status to Left the school with the date and the reason. Their portal account is a separate thing: switch it off in Staff.

My leave is in the menu of everyone whose account is linked to a staff record. It shows how many days of each kind of leave they have left this year, lets them ask for leave (the first and last day, who covers their work, and why) and cancel a request, or approved leave that has not started, and shows the main parts of their own record. HR's private notes are never shown there.

Staff leave is where requests are decided, by anyone allowed to approve leave (the HR Officer and the head teacher); the number beside it in the menu is how many are waiting. Each request shows the person's balance after it. Nobody decides their own leave: a request from the HR Officer waits for the head teacher or the School Admin. The person is told on the bell either way. Below are who is on leave now or soon, and what was decided.

  • Days are working days, Monday to Friday, in a calendar year; public holidays are not taken off. Leave counts against the year its first day falls in. A request that would take more days than are left is refused, and approving checks again.
  • Kinds of leave (annual, casual, sick, maternity, paternity, study and unpaid to start with) and each one's days a year are set on Kinds of leave. 0 days means no set allowance, so it is never refused for want of days. A person's own allowance for a year (days carried over) is set on the Leave tab of their record.
  • For someone without an account, HR records the leave on their record; it is approved at once when the person recording it may approve leave.

Troubleshooting: staff records and leave

I cannot change my own record, allowance or leave.
That is on purpose: nobody in HR changes their own. Ask another member of HR, or the School Admin.
A member of staff does not see My leave or My payslips.
Their staff record is not linked to their portal account. Open the record, choose their account under Portal account and save.
A leave request is refused for too many days.
It would take more days than the person has left this year. Check their balance on the Leave tab; add days carried over there if they are due, or choose a kind of leave with no set allowance (0 days).
My own leave request is waiting and nobody can approve it.
Nobody decides their own leave. A request from the HR Officer waits for the head teacher or the School Admin.
A career entry is wrong.
Entries cannot be changed or deleted. Strike out the wrong one with the reason, then add the right one.

Staff attendance

Staff clock in and out on their own phones. Staff attendance (in the menu under Management, Staff) is the register; setting it up needs the right to manage staff attendance (the HR Officer role has it; the head teacher may see the register). Only staff with a staff record can clock in, so add the staff records first.

  • Setting up. In Settings, under Staff & access, open Staff attendance; stand at the school and press Use where this device is now, set how far from that point counts as the school (150 metres suits most), the time after which someone is late, and the work days, then turn it on. Absences count from that day.
  • The screen. Make the link for the screen and open it on a tablet or computer staff pass on the way in. It shows a QR code and six digits that change every 30 seconds. It needs no sign-in, and shows nothing else of the portal. The link is shown only once; making a new one stops the old one at once.
  • Clocking in. A member of staff scans the QR code with their phone's camera (or opens Clock in and types the six digits) and presses Clock in; the same to clock out. The phone's location is read at that moment only, and only how far from the school it was is kept. The first phone someone clocks in with becomes theirs; another phone is refused until you Reset it in Settings.
  • What it cannot do. No phone check is impossible to cheat: a location can be faked with an app, and indoors a phone can be a hundred metres out. The changing code is the strong check; the location and the one phone make cheating harder. You can choose the code only (where phones find their location badly) or the location only (the weakest).
  • The register shows each day: who is in and when (late in amber), who is absent, on approved leave, or not in yet, and a closed day. Mark in and Mark leaving record someone by hand, with a note: staff without a portal account, or a phone that would not work. A time made on a phone is never changed by hand, and nobody marks their own attendance by hand.
  • Corrections. On Clock in, staff ask to put right a day in the last month they could not clock (a flat phone, a school trip). The request waits at the top of the register; approving it records the time as corrected, and they are told either way. Nobody decides their own.
  • The month is a grid of everyone's days with their totals, and Download CSV takes it to a spreadsheet. Closed days (public holidays, a mid-term break) are added in Settings, and nobody is absent on them.

Troubleshooting: staff attendance

"Your phone says it is … m from the school", or "Your phone is only sure of where it is to within … m".
The phone's location is outside the set distance from the school's point, or too rough to tell. Indoors, phones can be a hundred metres out: step outside or near a window and try again, and turn on the phone's precise location. If it happens to everyone, widen the distance in Settings → Staff attendance, or choose the code only.
"Your phone did not give its location".
The browser was not allowed to read the location. Allow location for the page (the padlock beside the address), then try again.
"This is not the phone you clock in with".
The first phone someone clocks in with becomes theirs. For a new or changed phone, press Reset beside their name in Settings → Staff attendance; their next clock-in makes the new phone theirs.
"That code is not the one on the school's screen now".
The six digits change every 30 seconds: type the one on the screen now, or scan the QR code. If the screen shows nothing, its link may have been replaced: make a new link in Settings and open it on the screen again.
Someone has no Clock in in their menu.
Only staff with a staff record linked to their account can clock in, and only once staff attendance is turned on.
Times look an hour or more out.
Each school keeps its own time zone, set by Beacon when the school was created (Lagos time unless the school is elsewhere). Ask Beacon support to change it.
Someone could not clock in on a day.
They ask for a correction on Clock in (for any day in the last month); someone else approves it from the register. Or mark them in by hand with a note.

Staff roles

Staff roles, in Settings, are the named bundles of permissions described in {{ page('roles-permissions', 'Roles, permissions and scope') }}. Each role's card shows its level in the school, how much it allows (a bar, and the number of permissions), the first few things it allows and who holds it. Find a role searches names, descriptions and permissions.

+ New role and Edit open the role in a pop-up: its name, level and what it is for, then its permissions grouped by area, with a search, a count of what is ticked in each area and Tick all for an area. An existing role also lists the staff who hold it; a name opens that person's pop-up on top. Saving keeps the pop-up open on the saved role; closing it brings the page up to date.

Two further pages exist mainly for checking your own work, reached by typing their address rather than a button in the menu (so do not worry if you never notice them): the permissions catalogue lists every permission that exists in the whole system, grouped by area, so you can see exactly what a role could carry before you build one; scopes lists every account in the school that is currently limited to particular classes, subjects or banks, all in one place.

The Administration page

The older Administration page, a few counts with links to staff, roles, controls and the activity log, still opens from its address, but everything on it now lives in Settings.

Messages

Messages has two tabs. Staff is a private inbox between members of staff — pick a colleague, write a note, attach a file if you need to; a parent or a student never sees it. Parents holds the conversations with parents (see {{ page('families', 'Parents') }}). A small number by the link in the navigation rail shows how many messages you have not opened yet.

Birthdays

Two days before a student's birthday, the staff who teach that student's class see a notification in their bell — for example "Ada Obi (JSS 1) turns 12 on Fri 11 Oct". Staff count as a class's teachers when they have access to that class and may write report card comments. Nothing needs setting up: the first time anyone opens an admin page each day, the school checks for birthdays coming up, and none is sent twice. It reads the date of birth on each student's record, so a student whose date is missing or not a readable date gets no reminder.

Birthday texts to parents

The school can also text each parent a greeting on their child's birthday. The School Admin switches it on under Email & SMS › Birthday texts, which shows what a parent receives. Its words are changed like every other message's, on Message wording.

  • The wording. {first_name} becomes the student's first name and {school} the school's name. The standard message: "Happy birthday, Ada! Everyone at Bright Star Academy wishes you a wonderful day and a great year ahead." Always one text message.
  • Who. Active students enrolled in the current session, with a readable date of birth. The text goes to each linked parent's phone, or to the guardian phone on the student's record when no parent account has one, the same numbers as every other text.
  • When. On the birthday itself, once, between 7:00 and 20:00 school time, from the first visit to the portal that day. Never a day late and never at night. A 29 February birthday is greeted on 28 February in other years.
  • The record. Each greeting is in the notice log as "Birthday greeting", sent or failed, with the number it went to.
A student's parents were not texted on the birthday.
Look the student up in the notice log. No entry: the date of birth is missing or not readable (write it as 14/10/2012), the student is not enrolled in the current session, or birthday texts were off. A failed entry says why: usually no phone number on file, or SMS not working (Check the connection on Email & SMS).
A birthday on a weekend or holiday was missed.
Greetings start from the first visit to the portal that day. The platform runs a daily timer that sends them even when nobody visits; if one was missed, tell the platform operator.

Who is online now

Online now, in Settings, shows how many staff, students and parents are signed in and active, then each of them with when they were last seen — useful, for example, to know whether the person you want to message is at their screen right now. Refresh brings it up to date; someone who stops using the portal drops off on their own after a few minutes.

Activity and notice logs

Activity & notice logs, in Settings, has two tabs side by side. Activity records who did what, to which record, from which address, and whether it went through — every release, every account change, every sign-in, and every refused attempt, including someone trying to open a workspace that is not theirs. Notices to parents is every email and text the school tried to send a parent, and whether it got through. Each tab opens on its own, so looking at one never slows down by reading the other.

Both show fifty records at a time, newest first and grouped by day; Older and Newer at the foot page through the rest. The search finds a person, an action, a student, an email address or a phone number across the whole log, and Refused only (or Not delivered) shows just what went wrong. Download (CSV) gives the whole activity log as a spreadsheet. Some actions also notify School Admins directly.

A student's own notice log opens in a pop-up from their record (Notice log): every attempt to tell their guardians something by email or SMS (a new assignment, a fee charged, a released report card or exam timetable, a store purchase collected), whether it went through or not, so you can answer "did she get it?" yourself. It pages and searches the same way. Payment receipts keep their own version of this on each receipt's own page.

Controls and locks

Controls & alerts, in Settings, gathers what needs a senior person's attention: Alerts (open one to go to what it is about, or mark it read), unread Messages, and the Review queue of things raised automatically, which the School Admin resolves. In the Entrance examination workspace it also lets a question bank — during a live exam, say — be frozen against changes, with a reason, until it is unlocked; in the School workspace it lists the subject locks.

How the portal looks

School profile sets the school's name, motto, logo, brand colours and sign-in photographs — everything a receipt, a report card and every page of the portal wear as their own. School Admin only: changing what the school is called or how it looks is kept out of ordinary staff reach.

Email and SMS

Email & SMS is where the school connects its own mail server and BulkSMS Nigeria account, so every automatic notice — a new report card, a payment, a released timetable — reaches a guardian on those channels too, not only in-app. School Admin only, since whoever holds these accounts can send a message that looks like it comes from the school.

Message wording

Message wording, in Settings, lists every email and text message parents receive from the school: a fee charged, a payment, a receipt, a report card or timetable released, a message from the school, a store collection, a birthday, a password reset and an entrance result. Each starts with the platform's standard words; the School Admin can rewrite any of them in the school's own voice.

  • Edit opens the message with what a parent would receive beside it. The parts in curly brackets, such as {child} or {amount}, are filled in for each parent; click one under the message to insert it. Only that message's own placeholders can be used.
  • Every text message is one text, at most 160 characters, so a school never pays for two. The count under the message is checked with a long name. Emoji cannot be used, and the naira sign, curly quotes and accents are sent as plain N, quotes and letters, which keeps a text in the cheapest form. If a very long name still pushes a text over, it is shortened at a word with "..." rather than sent as two.
  • Use the standard words puts the platform's words back; save to go back to them.
  • The password-reset email must keep its {link}, or nobody could reset a password.
"Shorten it so it costs one text".
With a long name filled in, the text would pass 160 characters. Shorten the words, or leave out a placeholder such as {school} (parents already see the school's sender name).
"This message can use only these in curly brackets".
A placeholder was misspelt or belongs to another message. Use the ones listed under the message.
A text arrived with "..." at the end.
The names in it were long enough to pass one text, so it was shortened. Shorten the wording a little.

The panel at the bottom right

Every member of staff sees a small panel at the bottom right of each page, with up to two cards.

  • What's new lists the latest changes to the portal in plain language, each with a link to the guide page that explains it. It shows only the changes you have not yet closed. Closing it hides those changes for you in this browser; another person sees their own.
  • Set up your school shows the setup steps for a new school (classes, subjects, students and fees) with their progress. Closing it hides it for the whole school, as it always has.
  • Minimise folds a card to its title bar. Your choice is remembered in this browser.

The panel opens minimised for What's new, so it does not cover the page when you arrive; click its title to read it. Each card has a capped height, and a long list scrolls inside the card rather than pushing the panel over the page. The setup checklist opens expanded for a new school.

The version of the portal you are running is shown at the bottom of the sign-in page, under the sign-in form. It is read from the release notes, the same source as What's new, so the number on the sign-in page always matches the newest release listed in What's new.

So that the system stays fast for every school, the panel does not look anything up each time you open a page. What's new is kept in your browser. The setup progress is kept in your browser too, and refreshed about every fifteen minutes, so a step you finish can take up to that long to show as done.
