Results and the five kinds of work (Assignments, Projects, Tests, Practice Tests and Examinations) share one page: Academics in the menu. Every kind except Practice feeds the same term result once marked (see {{ page('results-report-cards', 'Results and report cards') }}).

Finding your way around Academics

Across the top are the sections you can open — Results, Assignments, Projects, Tests, Practice tests and Examinations — with the one thing to create in that section on the right (for example New assignment or Enter offline scores).

Below them is a row of classes, each with its count; All classes lists everything you can see, with a Class column. The chosen class stays chosen when you switch section.

Everything opens in place: choosing, searching, saving, deleting and releasing load just the part that changes, with a small spinner while it does. The address bar still changes, so Back and bookmarks work as before, and a PDF or print view still opens on its own.

Setting new work, step by step

New assignment, New project, New test, New practice test and New examination are guided forms. They ask one thing at a time: Class and subject first, then the Details (title, dates and instructions), then for an assignment its Format (written or CBT, and any time limit), and for assignments and projects the Students it goes to. Next only moves on once the open step is filled in properly, and Back returns to an earlier one. A test, practice test or examination is created first, and its questions are added in the pop-up that follows (see Adding and editing questions).

Editing an assignment or a project opens over the list in a pop-up with the same steps. Click any step to jump to it, change what you need and Save changes from any of them; the pop-up closes and the list shows the change. Cancel, the × or a click outside closes it without saving.

Issuing work to a whole class or to chosen students

On the new-work form, the Students section has two choices. Whole class is the default for new work: every student enrolled in the chosen class receives it, including students who join the class later when the work is issued to them. Selected students shows the list of students, so you can tick only the ones who should receive it. The form tells you how many students the whole class has before you save.

Assignments

Assignments can be written work you mark by hand, or a quiz taken on-screen and marked automatically, timed or not. Open the list, choose New assignment, and set the class, subject, session and term it belongs to, which students it applies to (see the section above), and — for a quiz — the questions themselves (a text, four options and the right one, exactly like a question bank question). Once students have started work, opening one assignment from the list shows every student's own answer or score, ready to mark or check.

Projects

Projects are practical, longer-running tasks — a model to build, a report to write — with their own due date. There is no on-screen quiz for a project: a student is simply marked as having completed it or not, with a score and a remark you add by hand once you have seen the work. Each project card has its own Delete button, for whoever may edit that class and subject's projects.

Deleting and restoring work

Work that nobody has marked can be deleted. It leaves the current list and goes to the deleted list, which you open with Show deleted at the top of the page. From there, Restore puts it back on the current list, and nothing about it is lost.

An assignment or project that already has marks, scores or submissions cannot be deleted. Its marks are part of each student's term result, and a report card must still add up after the school closes the term. The portal refuses the delete and the work stays on the list. If the work was given by mistake, leave it on the list and tell the class teacher or the School Admin.

Deleting work never changes a report card that has already been released. The lists show only current work in the term results, so removing an unmarked item simply means it is no longer counted.

Tests and Examinations: two separate shelves for the same kind of work

Tests and Examinations work in exactly the same way — build them the same way you build an assignment's quiz, question by question — and both count fully toward a student's official term result, unlike practice below. The only difference between them is which list a piece of work sits on and which permission controls it, so a school can tell its own smaller, in-term tests apart from its bigger, formal end-of-term examinations, and decide separately who may create or edit each kind. Put a piece of work under whichever of the two your school calls it; nothing about how it behaves changes either way.

Adding and editing questions

On the Tests, Practice tests and Examinations lists, Questions (or the title) opens that piece of work in a pop-up over the list. At the top are its class, subject and term, whether students can see it (Open to students or Draft, with the button that changes it), and four figures: the number of questions, the total marks, the time allowed and the time that leaves for each question. Below them, three tabs:

  • Questions shows each question the way a student sees it, with its four options and the right answer marked in green. Edit opens the question where it is; Delete removes it after asking. The ↑ and ↓ arrows move a question, and a bar then offers Save the new order (or Undo).
  • Add a question takes the question, an optional instruction and picture, the four options and the marks. Tick the circle beside the correct option. After you save, the same tab is ready for the next question, so you can add a whole paper without leaving the pop-up.
  • Settings changes the title, time allowed, session, term and instructions. The class and subject stay as they were set.

A test can only be opened to students once it has at least one question. When you close the pop-up, the list underneath shows the new question count.

Practice tests

Practice is a self-study bank: nothing a student does there is ever written to the database — no attempt, no answers, no score — so it can never reach a term result or hold back a report card. Students can take and retake it as often as they like, and every run serves the questions in a fresh order.

Telling parents

New work and graded work notify the student and every linked parent in-app automatically; a guardian's email is also used where the school has set it up (see Email & SMS).
