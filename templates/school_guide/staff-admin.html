Running the school itself — its staff, its roles, its look, and who did what.

The top bar

Along the top of every page: Guide and Messages (with a count of unread messages), the notifications bell, and your own name and photo. Click your name to open a small menu with Sign out. The name at the top is where to look to sign out; the side menu no longer has it.

The side menu

The menu opens while you hover over it. A small box on its right edge, at the bottom of the screen, keeps it open: click it once and the menu stays open on every page until you click it again. While the menu is open from hovering, that box steps aside so it never covers an item.

Each entry has its own icon, drawn for what it opens, so the closed menu can be read at a glance: a compass for Overview, two speech bubbles for Messages, a mortarboard for Students, a parent with a child for Parents, a teacher at a board for Teachers, rows of desks for Classes, a register with ticks for Attendance, an A+ for Academics, a calendar with a clock for the exam timetable, a document with a rosette for Report cards, stairs for Promotion, coins for Collections, a card for Payments, a ledger for Student accounts, a price tag for Fee structure, books on a shelf for the Library, a gear for Settings and two arrows for Switch workspace.

Settings

Settings, at the foot of the menu, holds everything that sets the portal up, decides who can use it and records what happened: the school profile, academic sessions, staff and their roles, report card settings, Email & SMS, online payments, receipts, controls and alerts, the activity log and who is online. You see only what your role allows.

  • The overview comes first. Worth a look lists anything that needs attention (online payments not connected, the head's signature missing, alerts left open); below it is every setting by group, each with its state at a glance, such as which session is current or whether Email & SMS is the school's own account.
  • The list on the left stays on every Settings page. Each setting opens beside it, in place, without leaving Settings. Find a setting narrows the list as you type (Enter opens the first match), and a dot marks a setting that needs a look.
  • Each page opens with its name, what it is for and its state, plus its main action where it has one: + Add staff on Staff, + New role on Staff roles.
  • The entrance examination's set-up is a workspace of its own, so it opens as its own page (its entry carries a small arrow).
Four areas are kept for the School Admin alone — the account created when the school itself was set up, never a role you build or grant: Staff and Staff roles, Email & SMS, Online payments and the School profile. Nobody else can be given access to them, no matter what permissions their role carries; if you cannot see them in Settings, that is why, and it is normal for every account except the School Admin's own.

Staff

Teachers (in the menu under People) shows who teaches what. The figures across the top count the gaps: classes with no class teacher, and subjects offered but taught by nobody. View it By class (each class with its class teacher and subject teachers) or By teacher, and tick Only what needs a teacher to see the gaps alone. Change duties opens a pop-up with the classes they are class teacher of and the subjects they teach.

Staff, in Settings, is everyone who signs in to the school's side of the portal: teachers, the bursar, the secretary, the librarian. Each person has a card with their name, username, whether they are active or suspended, their roles, their contact details and when they last signed in, and it says in plain words what they can reach: full access (the School Admin), a teacher's own classes and subjects, a limited area, or standard access from their roles.

  • The figures at the top (Everyone, Can sign in, Suspended) are also the filters, and the search finds anyone by name, username or email.
  • + Add staff and Manage open the person in a pop-up over the list.
  • The ⋯ menu on a card sends them a message, resets their password (they get a new one-time password) or suspends or reactivates their access. Suspending stops their sign-in straight away; their record stays, and they can be reactivated at any time. You cannot suspend or reset your own account.

The staff pop-up has four tabs, all one form, so moving between them keeps what you typed:

  • Profile: their name, username, contact details and photo. The username is how they sign in and cannot be changed once made.
  • Roles: tick every job they do, grouped from the most senior down (head teacher, class teacher, subject teacher), with a summary that updates as you tick. See beside a role opens that role in a second pop-up on top, to check or change what it allows; closing it brings you back to the staff member exactly as you left them.
  • Teaching duties, for teachers: the class they are class teacher of, and the subjects they teach in each class (and, in SSS, which department). See {{ page('roles-permissions', 'Roles, permissions and scope') }}.
  • Limits, rarely needed: keeps anyone else to chosen classes, subjects, sessions or question banks.
  • Overrides (the School Admin only) gives single permissions for the exceptional case.

The bar at the foot saves, cancels, resets their password or suspends them. When you add someone, or reset a password, their username and one-time password show in the same pop-up, ready to print; it is shown once, and must be changed at their first sign-in. Nobody, not even the School Admin, can see a password, only reset it.

Staff roles

Staff roles, in Settings, are the named bundles of permissions described in {{ page('roles-permissions', 'Roles, permissions and scope') }}. Each role's card shows its level in the school, how much it allows (a bar, and the number of permissions), the first few things it allows and who holds it. Find a role searches names, descriptions and permissions.

+ New role and Edit open the role in a pop-up: its name, level and what it is for, then its permissions grouped by area, with a search, a count of what is ticked in each area and Tick all for an area. An existing role also lists the staff who hold it; a name opens that person's pop-up on top. Saving keeps the pop-up open on the saved role; closing it brings the page up to date.

Two further pages exist mainly for checking your own work, reached by typing their address rather than a button in the menu (so do not worry if you never notice them): the permissions catalogue lists every permission that exists in the whole system, grouped by area, so you can see exactly what a role could carry before you build one; scopes lists every account in the school that is currently limited to particular classes, subjects or banks, all in one place.

The Administration page

The older Administration page, a few counts with links to staff, roles, controls and the activity log, still opens from its address, but everything on it now lives in Settings.

Messages

Messages has two tabs. Staff is a private inbox between members of staff — pick a colleague, write a note, attach a file if you need to; a parent or a student never sees it. Parents holds the conversations with parents (see {{ page('families', 'Parents') }}). A small number by the link in the navigation rail shows how many messages you have not opened yet.

Birthday reminders

Two days before a student's birthday, the staff who teach that student's class see a notification in their bell — for example "Ada Obi (JSS 1) turns 12 on Fri 11 Oct". Staff count as a class's teachers when they have access to that class and may write report card comments. Nothing needs setting up: the first time anyone opens an admin page each day, the school checks for birthdays coming up, and none is sent twice. It reads the date of birth on each student's record, so a student whose date is missing or not a readable date gets no reminder.

Who is online now

Online now, in Settings, shows how many staff, students and parents are signed in and active, then each of them with when they were last seen — useful, for example, to know whether the person you want to message is at their screen right now. Refresh brings it up to date; someone who stops using the portal drops off on their own after a few minutes.

Activity log

The Activity log, in Settings, records who did what, to which record, from which address, and whether it went through — every release, every account change, every sign-in, and every refused attempt, including someone trying to open a workspace that is not theirs. The newest 250 records are grouped by day; the search finds a person, an action or an address as you type, and Refused only shows just what was turned away. Download (CSV) gives the whole log as a spreadsheet. Some actions also notify School Admins directly.

A link from that page, or from a student's own record, opens the Notice log — every attempt to tell a guardian something by email or SMS (a new assignment, a fee charged, a released report card or exam timetable), whether it actually went through or not, so you can answer "did she get it?" yourself. Payment receipts keep their own version of this on each receipt's own page.

Controls and locks

Controls & alerts, in Settings, gathers what needs a senior person's attention: Alerts (open one to go to what it is about, or mark it read), unread Messages, and the Review queue of things raised automatically, which the School Admin resolves. In the Entrance examination workspace it also lets a question bank — during a live exam, say — be frozen against changes, with a reason, until it is unlocked; in the School workspace it lists the subject locks.

How the portal looks

School profile sets the school's name, motto, logo, brand colours and sign-in photographs — everything a receipt, a report card and every page of the portal wear as their own. School Admin only: changing what the school is called or how it looks is kept out of ordinary staff reach.

Email and SMS

Email & SMS is where the school connects its own mail server and BulkSMS Nigeria account, so every automatic notice — a new report card, a payment, a released timetable — reaches a guardian on those channels too, not only in-app. School Admin only, since whoever holds these accounts can send a message that looks like it comes from the school.

The panel at the bottom right

Every member of staff sees a small panel at the bottom right of each page, with up to two cards.

  • What's new lists the latest changes to the portal in plain language, each with a link to the guide page that explains it. It shows only the changes you have not yet closed. Closing it hides those changes for you in this browser; another person sees their own.
  • Set up your school shows the setup steps for a new school (classes, subjects, students and fees) with their progress. Closing it hides it for the whole school, as it always has.
  • Minimise folds a card to its title bar. Your choice is remembered in this browser.

The panel opens minimised for What's new, so it does not cover the page when you arrive; click its title to read it. Each card has a capped height, and a long list scrolls inside the card rather than pushing the panel over the page. The setup checklist opens expanded for a new school.

The version of the portal you are running is shown at the bottom of the sign-in page, under the sign-in form. It is read from the release notes, the same source as What's new, so the number on the sign-in page always matches the newest release listed in What's new.

So that the system stays fast for every school, the panel does not look anything up each time you open a page. What's new is kept in your browser. The setup progress is kept in your browser too, and refreshed about every fifteen minutes, so a step you finish can take up to that long to show as done.
