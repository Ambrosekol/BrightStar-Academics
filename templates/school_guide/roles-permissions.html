Every account has one or more roles, each a named bundle of individual permissions (things like "View students" or "Release timetables"). Whether you see a link in the navigation, or can open a page, or get refused with "you don't have permission" — all of it comes down to which permissions your roles give you.

The School Admin role

Every school has a School Admin role that holds every permission and is never limited to particular classes or subjects. If you can see everything described in this guide and more, you probably hold this role, or you are a platform operator visiting the school (see below).

Who is above whom

The portal follows the usual order of a Nigerian school, from the top down:

  1. Proprietor / School Admin: everything, everywhere.
  2. Executive & office: the bursar, admissions officer, secretary, HR, librarian and other office roles. Each sees their own area (fees, admissions, records, the library) across the whole school.
  3. Head teacher (or principal): every class and subject. Checks, approves and releases any result, and decides who teaches what on the Teachers page.
  4. Class teacher: in charge of a class. Sees every subject of it, takes its register, writes its report card comments and releases its results once the subject teachers have sent them.
  5. Subject teacher: teaches chosen subjects in chosen classes. Sets work and tests, records the scores and sends them to the class teacher. Cannot release them.

Every role has a level (set on the role itself under Roles), which puts it in this order. The staff form lists roles from the most senior down.

Teaching duties: which class and which subject

A teacher's role says what they may do; their teaching duties say where. When you register a teacher (or open their staff page), the Teaching duties section asks two things:

  • Class teacher of: tick the class (or classes) they are in charge of.
  • Subjects they teach: one row per subject and class, for example Mathematics in JSS 1. In SSS classes a row can be limited to one department (Science, Art or Commercial), for a teacher who takes, say, Mathematics for the Science students only.

Once a teacher has duties, those duties are all they see: the class list, the students, the subjects on every form, the Results page and the work pages all follow them. A teacher with no duties yet can still reach every class, so add them when you register a teacher.

A subject teacher sets their own work: tests, practice tests, examinations, assignments (written or CBT) and projects, and the questions in them, for each subject in each class they were given. On the new-work forms, choosing a class narrows the Subject list to what they teach there (and picks it when there is only one), and anything else is refused on saving. A class teacher can set work in every subject of their own class. Deleting work is for roles that carry the delete permission, such as the Head Teacher.

One person can be the class teacher of one class and teach subjects in others: tick their class under Class teacher of and add a row for each subject elsewhere (give them the Class Teacher role, which includes everything a subject teacher does). The class teacher's own work stays with their own class: they take its register, open and comment on its report cards, and release its results. In the classes where they only teach a subject they record that subject's marks and send them to that class's class teacher; they can see its attendance summary, but cannot take its register, open its report cards or release its results.

The Teachers page shows every class with its class teacher and subject teachers, and the subjects nobody has been given yet. The School Admin and anyone with the Assign teaching duties permission (the Head Teacher role has it) can change a teacher's duties there. A head teacher can only change the duties of staff below them, and only in the classes they look after. Creating an account or choosing its role stays with the School Admin.

Ready-made roles

A new school starts with nineteen ready-made roles under Roles — Head Teacher, Class Teacher, Subject Teacher, Finance Manager (the bursar), Admissions Officer, Librarian, Report Card Officer and more — each already carrying a sensible bundle of permissions for that job. You can edit what a role carries, or build your own from scratch. Roles and accounts are themselves managed under {{ page('staff-admin', 'Administration') }}, which — like Email & SMS, Online Payments and School Profile — is kept for the School Admin alone, so nobody can hand themselves or a colleague a permission they should not have.

Other limits: some classes, sessions or banks

For teachers, use teaching duties (above). For anyone else, an account can still be limited to chosen classes, sessions, subjects or question banks under Limits, for example a head of the nursery section limited to the Crèche and Nursery classes, or someone managing question banks limited to particular banks. Class and subject limits are ignored for an account that has teaching duties. This is set per person, in the Limits tab of their pop-up in Staff (in Settings). It is why two teachers looking at the same page — Students, Attendance, Results — see different rows: each sees only what their scope allows.

If a page or a button you expect to see is missing, it is almost always a permission or a scope, not a fault. Ask whoever manages Administration at your school to check what your account's role and scope allow.

The bulk-import permission

The permission Run bulk imports (school.bulk_import) can be given to any role. It lets that role run all three bulk import steps (students, enrolment history and past results) without also holding each step's own permission. Its holder can also create an earlier session during the history step, and archive a graduate. It grants nothing else: the rest of the school stays as the role allows. Every import is recorded in the audit log with who ran it, the file name and the counts.

Finding a permission quickly

On a staff member's page, under Overrides, a search box filters the permissions as you type. It is forgiving: capital letters, accents, the order of words and small spelling mistakes do not matter, so "student delete" and "finence" both find what you are looking for. Overrides are for exceptions only. Ordinary access should come from roles.

New accounts and temporary passwords

A new staff, student or parent account is always created with a one-time password, shown once when the account is made. The first sign-in forces a real password to be chosen before anything else opens. Nobody — not even the School Admin — can see what that password was afterwards, only reset it to a new one-time password.
