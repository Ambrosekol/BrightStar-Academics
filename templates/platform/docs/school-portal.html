Everything a school's staff use, at the school's own address. This page explains who staff are, what they are allowed to do, and how that is enforced. The list of every permission and role is generated on the permissions reference.

Two workspaces

A staff member signs in and lands on the workspace home ({{ route('admin_workspace_home') }}), where they choose Entrance Examination or School. The choice is remembered in the session and enforced by a request hook in {{ file('app.py') }} (enforce_admin_workspace_boundary): the paths of one workspace are refused from the other, so a person cannot wander from entrance administration into student records by editing an address. Which paths belong to which workspace is one function, _admin_workspace_for_path.

Roles, permissions and scope

Permission
a single ability, e.g. school.results.release→ Role
a named bundle of permissions→ Account
one or more roles, plus optional overrides→ Scope
limits it to certain classes, subjects or banks
  • The catalogue. Every permission is defined once in ADMIN_PERMISSION_DEFS ({{ file('core/security.py') }}). At every start, init_admin_security in {{ file('app.py') }} seeds the catalogue into the school's {{ table('permissions') }} table without ever creating an account.
  • The School Admin role. The system role (admin_types.is_system = 1) has every permission and is unrestricted by scope. It is found by that flag, never by name: a custom role that happens to be called "School Admin" must not be mistaken for it and handed everything. Platform operators who enter a school hold this role.
  • Role presets. A new school gets seventeen ready-made roles (Primary Class Teacher, Finance Manager, Librarian, Report Card Officer and so on), seeded once; edits a school makes are kept.
  • Administration is the School Admin role's own. Creating, editing, deactivating or resetting another administrator's account, and managing roles, permissions and scopes themselves ({{ file('blueprints/administration/routes.py') }}) all require is_school_admin directly - the same gate as Academic Sessions and Promotion - rather than a grantable permission, so this is never delegated to a custom role. admin_can_delegate_roles still exists in the account-creation form's own code as a second check, but is now unreachable in practice since nothing past the school-admin gate can be a non-system account.
  • Overrides. An individual account can carry permission overrides independent of role ({{ table('admin_permissions') }}).
  • Scope. A class teacher is limited to their own class; a college subject teacher can additionally be limited to a subject; a bank manager to particular question banks. Scopes are rows in {{ table('admin_scopes') }} and are checked by admin_scope_allows. Denials are audited (scope_denied).

How a route is guarded

@app.route('/admin/school/results')
@admin_required
def admin_school_results(): ...

admin_required ({{ file('core/security.py') }}) looks up the route's endpoint name in ADMIN_ENDPOINT_PERMISSIONS, checks the signed-in account holds that permission (falling back to admin.access for an unmapped endpoint), extracts a scope from the URL's arguments where the endpoint has one, forces a password change if the account is on a temporary password, and audits a refusal. Routes still do finer checks themselves: for example, a results route also asks whether this account may see this class ({{ file('blueprints/school/helpers.py') }}, _school_class_allowed).

An admin endpoint with no entry in the map is only guarded by admin.access, which nearly every staff account holds. When you add an admin route, add it to the map. A contract test helps, but it cannot know which permission you meant.

What the School workspace covers

The School workspace is the largest part of the product; each area has its own page.

  • Sessions, classes, subjects, students, enrolment, promotion, assignments, projects, tests, practice, examinations, exam timetables, attendance, results and report cards: {{ page('academics', 'Academics') }}.
  • Fees, payments and receipts: {{ page('finance', 'Finance and receipts') }}. Library books and loans: {{ file('blueprints/library/routes.py') }}.
  • Parent accounts and feedback, and the school's own email and SMS: {{ page('families', 'Students, parents and messages') }}.
  • Branding and numbering: {{ page('branding-numbering', 'Identity and numbering') }}.

The new-school setup checklist

The School workspace home page ({{ route('admin_school_home') }}) shows a grouped, ordered checklist of everything a new school puts in order: its profile and look, the academic session, classes and subjects (and which classes take which), staff, students and parents, fee items and billing, online payments, the receipt signature, the report card settings, attendance, the exam timetable, email/SMS and the library ({{ file('blueprints/school/onboarding.py') }}). The essentials are marked apart from the optional extras. Each step's state is read live from a plain count of the school's own active rows — never a stored flag — so it can never disagree with what the school has actually done. A school may dismiss it early ({{ route('admin_school_onboarding_dismiss') }}, one {{ table('school_settings') }} row) and bring it back ({{ route('admin_school_onboarding_show') }}); it disappears by itself once every step is done.

The Entrance workspace is described in {{ page('entrance', 'Entrance examinations') }}.

Top of the Class

On the Overview's second row, beside the People cards (Students and Parents), the same page shows the highest-scoring student in each class for the current term: one row per class, its top student's overall percentage and letter grade, reusing class_term_stats ({{ file('blueprints/school/report_card_data.py') }}) - the same aggregate the report-card pages already compute, never a separate calculation of its own. Which classes appear is scoped exactly like every other class-facing page: _school_class_allowed ({{ file('blueprints/school/helpers.py') }}) shows every active class to a School Admin and only a scoped teacher's own classes to them. A class with nobody ready yet (an official term result needs every one of a student's subjects released) shows as waiting rather than being silently left out.

Archiving: leavers keep their record

A student who leaves is archived, not deleted. Archiving sets the student's active flag to 0 and records archived_at, the administrator who archived them, and the reason. Because every current list, count and sign-in already filters on active, the archived student drops out of them all at once, and the same checks stop their login. Their open presence sessions are ended in the same step.

The archive and restore logic is one helper, {{ file('blueprints/school/helpers.py') }} (archive_students), used by the class register, the student page and the history import. Already archived students are left as they are, so the first archive's date and reason are never overwritten. A School Admin can restore a student; the archived list and each archived record are for the School Admin only ({{ route('admin_school_students_archived') }}).

Parents are deliberately not cut off: the parent dashboard, the child's record and the fee account include archived children, so the family keeps its history. Only the child's own sign-in is revoked.

What's new, the setup checklist and browser caching

Release notes are announced from one file, {{ file('release_notes.json') }}, read by {{ file('core/release_notes.py') }}. Each item has an audience: staff, family or student. Staff items keep a link to the matching guide page; family and student items are complete on their own and never carry one, because families cannot open the staff guide.

  • Administrators see the staff items in the panel at the bottom right of every admin page ({{ file('blueprints/admin_dock.py') }}, {{ file('templates/_admin_dock.html') }}).
  • Students and parents see their own items on their dashboard ({{ file('blueprints/family_notices.py') }}, {{ file('templates/_family_notice.html') }}). A student never receives a parent's item, and the reverse.
  • The database is not asked for any of this on an ordinary page. The server sends the notes for the audience, and the browser decides what to show, from the version it last saw for that account (stored in local storage). Closing a notice is a browser setting, so it does not reach another device.
  • The setup checklist is the one thing read from the database on request: {{ route('admin_school_onboarding_card') }} answers with the school's progress. The browser keeps that answer for fifteen minutes before asking again. Closing the checklist is a real change and is saved for the whole school.
  • The sign-in footer shows the version the platform is running, read from the newest release in {{ file('release_notes.json') }} by {{ file('blueprints/platform_version.py') }} and shown by {{ file('templates/login.html') }}. It needs no database read, and it always matches What's new, because both come from the same file.

The staff guide

/admin/guide ({{ file('blueprints/school/guide.py') }}) is a plain-language, hand-written user guide for the people who use the portal — not this documentation, which is for platform operators and generated from the code. It carries no permission of its own: an endpoint left out of ADMIN_ENDPOINT_PERMISSIONS falls back to admin.access, which nearly every staff account holds, so any signed-in admin can read the whole thing in either workspace without switching. Fifteen pages, in six groups that follow the order of a school year (getting started, setting up the school, students and families, academics, money and library, and the entrance workspace), with a search box (a plain substring match over the rendered text of every page, no code introspection) and an on-this-page outline built from its own headings. Its own contract test, {{ file('tests/current/test_guide_contract.py') }}, checks every link and slug the same way {{ file('tests/current/test_docs_contract.py') }} checks this documentation.

Accounts other people get

Students, parents and entrance candidates are created by staff, never self-registered. Each receives a generated one-time password and must change it at first sign-in (students and parents; candidates use the credentials printed for them). Student sign-in names come from the admission number; numbers themselves come from the school's numbering rules.
