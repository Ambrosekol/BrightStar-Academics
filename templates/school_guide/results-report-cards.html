A mark goes through the same journey everywhere it comes from — a test, an assignment, a manually entered score — before it ever reaches a student or parent.

Recorded, sent to the class teacher, released

Under Academics → Results, pick a class; its students are listed on the left, and choosing one shows their sessions and terms on the right, each with how many results are released. Choosing a term lists that term's marks with the step each is waiting for. The work is shared out the way a school does it:

  1. The subject teacher records the scores (Enter offline result), then sends them to the class teacher: Send to class teacher on one mark, Send all to class teacher on a student's term, or Send all my results for this class from the class's student list. The marks are then ready to release. A subject teacher only ever sees their own subjects, and in SSS only the students of the departments they teach.
  2. The class teacher sees every subject of their class, and releases the term to the student and parents. A mark that looks wrong can be sent back to its subject teacher to correct and send again. Only the class teacher of a class (or a head teacher, or the School Admin) can release it. In a class split into subdivisions, the class teacher of a subdivision comments on and releases the results of its own students; the class averages on the report card are still the whole class's.
  3. The head teacher can do all of this for any class, and sets the whole school's release date.

Behind the scenes a result still moves through four stages: recorded (entered), checked (verified), ready to release (approved) and released, and a head teacher can use the separate check and approve steps if the school wants a mark looked at twice. Only released results count toward a student's official term result and their report card; the earlier stages let more than one person check a mark before it becomes visible to anyone outside staff. Opening a student's own term has one button that releases every subject of it at once, instead of one at a time; going the other way, Release the whole class's term at once (from the students list, before you pick one) previews exactly who is ready — how many subjects, how many already released, how many still waiting — before releasing everyone who actually has something approved, in one step.

Entering offline scores

Enter offline scores (the button on Results, or this page) is for marks from paper tests and exams, and is made for working down a whole class. Choose the session, term and subject at the top and a class below; the class's students are listed on the left with the score each already has in that subject (only the students who take it, in SSS), and a bar shows how many are done. Choose a student, say whether they took the Test (or why they missed it), and type the scores the class's grading asks for (the Test, any typed-in CA parts and the Exam; each box starts at its share, such as out of 20); the percentage shows as you type. Save and next moves straight to the next student without a score and keeps the same maxima. A subject the class does not have yet can be created from the same bar. When the class is done, send the marks to the class teacher from Results.

When the class's grading scheme has typed-in CA parts (CA1, CA2...), the page shows a box for each, beside the Test and the Exam; each is optional (leave it empty if not taken yet) and has its own maximum.

How a term result is worked out

Each subject's term result is its continuous assessment (CA) plus its exam, out of 100, in the shares the level's grading scheme sets. Several items in one part (two tests, say) are added together before they are scaled to the part's share, so an extra test never pushes a subject past its share. Work that has been removed stops counting; the arithmetic is worked out fresh every time it is needed, from the results themselves, so it can never disagree with what is actually on record.

Grading and scores

Grading and scores (Settings, Results & report cards; the head teacher and the academic administrator) sets, for each level (Nursery, Primary, JSS, SSS) and for each session:

  • The score split: the Exam's share and up to four CA parts, each with a name, a share and where its marks come from: the class's tests, its assignments, its projects, or typed in by the teacher (CA1, CA2, CA3...). They must add up to 100; the total shows as you type. A test can be set to count as one of the typed-in parts (on the test's own page), so a CA2 sat on the computer counts as CA2.
  • The grade scale: bands from the top down, each with its lowest percentage, grade and remark. Start from A to F, WAEC's A1 to F9, or words for nursery (Excellent, Very good...), and change any band.
{{ shot('grading', 'Grading and scores: the session at the top, a tab for each level, the score split on the left (adding up to 100, with a tick) and the grade scale on the right.') }}

Each session keeps its own: saving changes the report cards of that session only, never an earlier one. A new session carries on the latest grading the school saved, until you save one of its own. Copy this level to every level fills the others with what is on screen. Until a school saves, every level uses the original split (its CA weights) and A to F.

The Third Term report: the session so far

A Third Term report card also shows the session so far: each subject's First, Second and Third Term marks, their average and grade, and the session average and position in the class. A term the student has no report card for is left out of their averages (a student who joined in the Second Term is averaged over two terms). In the report card studio, the Session so far block and the Session average, Session grade and Position for the session details show it on the school's own design; on First and Second Term cards they stay empty, so one design serves every term.

The broadsheet

The broadsheet (Broadsheet on Report cards) is a class's master score sheet: one row per student, one column per subject, then the total, the average, the position and the grade; under it each subject's average, highest and lowest. Choose a term, or Whole session for each subject's average over the terms; tick CA and Exam to see both under each subject. Unlike report cards it counts every mark recorded, released or not, so it can be checked before release; each student shows how many of their results are still waiting. Print prints it in landscape and Download CSV opens it in a spreadsheet. It is the class teacher's, like the report cards.

Testimonials, transcripts and leaving certificates

Documents (in the menu under Academics) is the register of every testimonial, transcript and leaving certificate issued, each with its own serial number, such as BSA/TST/2026/00001. + Issue a document asks only what the kind needs:

  • Testimonial: the student's conduct, the head's words on their character and any positions held.
  • Leaving certificate: the conduct and the reason for leaving.
  • Transcript: nothing more: one row per subject and one column per session the student has released results in, each the average of that session's terms, and the overall average.

The student's name, date of birth, classes, sessions and the dates they joined and left come from their record (the dates can be changed on the form). Students who have left are offered too. A document keeps exactly what it said when it was issued, so a copy printed later is the same; PDF prints it in the school's design (the documents studio, under Design studios). One issued by mistake is revoked with a reason: it stays in the register and prints marked REVOKED.

{{ shot('documents-register', 'Documents: the register of everything issued, by kind, with a PDF of each; a revoked one shows when and why.') }} {{ shot('document-issue', 'Issuing a testimonial: choose the kind, the student (type to narrow the list), then only what that kind asks for.') }}

A document's serial number starts with the same prefix as the school's receipt numbers, then the kind (TST, TRN or LVC) and the year, and counts up from 00001 each year for each kind.

Exam registration: WAEC, NECO and BECE

Exam registration lists a class's candidates for WAEC or NECO (SSS) or BECE (JSS) with their surname, names, sex, date of birth, state of origin and the subjects they take, and says what is missing on each record (a photograph, a date of birth that cannot be read...). Put it right on the student's record, then download Candidates (CSV) and Photographs (ZIP), each photograph a passport-size JPEG named to match its row, to carry into the exam body's own portal. Check the details against each candidate's birth certificate first.

Troubleshooting: grading, broadsheets, documents and exam registration

Grading and scores will not save: "They must add up to 100".
The Exam and every CA part of each level must add up to exactly 100 (the total beside Score split shows a tick when they do). Every level is checked when you save, so look at each tab: the message names the level.
I changed the grading, but last session's report cards did not change.
That is on purpose: grading is kept per session, so cards already given out never change. To change an earlier session, choose it in Session at the top of Grading and scores and save it there.
A new session is graded differently from what we set.
A new session uses the grading the school saved most recently, until it has its own; the line beside Session says which. Choose the new session and save it to give it its own.
The score sheet has no box for CA1 (or CA2...).
Only parts whose marks are typed in by the teacher get a box. Check the grading of the class's level (a JSS class follows the JSS tab) for the session you are entering. A part that comes from tests, assignments or projects is filled in from that work instead.
A test done on the computer counts as the Test, not as CA2.
Open the test and set Counts as to CA2. A test with no choice counts towards the Test part.
A student's grade looks wrong.
A mark gets the first band, from the top down, whose From % it reaches: with WAEC's scale 74 is B2 (70 to 74) and 75 is A1. The lowest band always starts at 0. Check you are looking at the right level and session.
The Third Term card has no "session so far" table.
It is only on Third Term cards. A First or Second Term is counted only if the student's card for that term was ready (all its results released). On the school's own design, add the Session so far block in the report card studio.
The broadsheet does not match the report cards.
The broadsheet counts every mark recorded, released or not, so it can be checked before release; a report card counts only released results. The waiting figure beside a student says how many of their results are not yet released.
I cannot see my class's broadsheet.
It follows the report card rule: a class teacher sees their own class; the head teacher, the academic administrator and the School Admin see every class.
A transcript is refused: "no released results yet".
A transcript lists only terms whose report card is ready. Release the term's results first.
A document says something wrong (a name, a date, a class).
A document keeps what it said when it was issued. Put the student's record right, Revoke the wrong document with a reason, and issue a new one; it gets the next serial number.
Documents print in the starting design, not ours.
They print from the saved design of their kind in the documents studio: press Save there. A change can take up to a minute to reach every copy.
Exam registration does not list a class.
WAEC and NECO list SSS classes and BECE lists JSS classes, going by each class's stage (set on Classes).
A candidate is marked "date of birth (age 6?)" or similar.
The date of birth on their record could not be read, or gives an age outside 9 to 30. Correct it on the student's record.
A candidate's photograph is not in the ZIP.
Add a photograph on the student's record. A file that is not a real picture is left out (the others still come).
An SSS candidate shows no subjects.
In SSS the subjects follow the student's department: set the department on their record, and check the class's subjects are given to that department.
A cell in the CSV starts with an apostrophe.
A cell that begins like a spreadsheet formula (=, +, -, @) gets an apostrophe in front, so a spreadsheet does not run it. Remove it in the spreadsheet if it really is part of the value.

Report cards

Report cards opens with the class chips across the top and the session and term beside them; choosing a class loads its students in place. Each student's comment and trait ratings are written in a small pop-up without leaving the page. View and PDF open the card in a new tab, ready to print, and a student's name opens their own pop-up.

{{ shot('reportcards', 'Report cards: choose the class with the chips; comments and ratings are written in a small pop-up.') }}

For a student in SSS 1 to 3, the report card also shows their department (Science, Art or Commercial), beside their class.

A student's report card for a term appears — to the student, a linked parent, and staff — the moment every one of that student's results for the term has been released, whether by hand or by a session's own release date. One result still waiting holds the whole card back. It shows the school's own branding, each subject's marks and grade, the overall percentage, the class average, and (once at least one has been rated) affective and psychomotor trait ratings, alongside the class teacher's comment and the head's signature.

Open Report cards from the navigation rail to see, for a class, session and term you choose, which students already have a ready card and which are still waiting (and why — which subject has not been released yet). From there you can open one student's card to read it exactly as they would see it, download it as a PDF, or — if every student in the class is ready — download the whole class's cards as one PDF at once, for printing or filing.

The School workspace's Overview page also shows, for the current term, the single highest-scoring student in each class you can see — a quick "who's leading" view, updated automatically as results are released, needing nothing set up.

Choosing a class, session and term across the top of the page loads that class's students right there, with a spinning indicator and no page reload. Beside each student, a class teacher uses Comment to write the comment and Rate traits to rate Punctuality, Neatness, Leadership and the like; each opens a small window, and can be used before results are even released. Whoever last changes a comment or a rating is credited for it; saving it unchanged never takes over someone else's. My signature, at the top right, opens a window to draw or upload your own signature.

When a card becomes ready, the parent is told automatically — an in-app alert, an email and a text message — that it is ready to view and download. Nobody has to remember to announce it.

Setting up how a report card looks

Two small pages decide what appears at the bottom of every report card in the school: Report card settings sets the head of school's title (Principal, Headmistress, and so on) and their signature image, once for the whole school. How a subject's mark is made up, and graded, is set under Grading and scores (see above). My signature is different: it is where any member of staff draws or uploads their own signature image, for use wherever their own name appears signed on a document. It opens as a window on the Report cards page.

The report card studio: the school's own design

Design studios (in the menu under Management, Design & print) opens the school's design tools: the ID card studio, the receipt studio (see Finance) and the report card studio. The report card studio needs the right to manage report card settings (the Report Card Officer and Head Teacher roles have it); there is also a Design the report card button on the Report cards page.

Both studios fill the whole screen, like a design program: the tools along the top, what to add on the left (Insert, Details, Images and Page tabs), the page in the middle, and the chosen item's settings, the layers and the versions on the right. A settings section folds away under its heading; Exit leaves, and the ⛶ button hides the browser's own bars. Designing needs a computer: on a phone, or in a window narrower than 1024 pixels, the studios ask you to use a computer instead.

  • Start from a template. Templates shows four complete report cards (Classic, Modern, Elegant, Bold) drawn in the school's own colours. Pick one, then change anything. Undo (Ctrl+Z) brings back what was there before.
  • The report blocks. The results table (choose its columns), the summary (total, percentage, grade, class average, position, highest and lowest in class), Attendance (times school opened, days present, days absent, attendance percentage), the trait ratings, the grading key and the class teacher's comment. Each has its own font, sizes, heading band, stripes, lines, corners and colours. A table's rows grow or shrink to fill its box, so the same design suits a class with eight subjects and one with sixteen.
  • The student's details. Name, admission number, class, gender, date of birth, age, parent or guardian, attendance, position, blood group, genotype, state of origin, religion and more: click one under Student details, or add a Detail and choose what it shows. Health records are never offered. A parent's or student's report card page only carries the details the design shows.
  • Everything else. Text in any of four fonts, shapes with fills, gradients and outlines, the photograph and the logo clipped to a circle or shape, the head's and the class teacher's signatures, a barcode of the admission number, the school's own images (shared with the ID card studio) as pictures or a watermark, and a second page (printed only when something is on it). Layers, alignment, snapping, a grid, zoom, copy and paste all work as in the ID card studio.
  • Preview, save, switch on. Preview PDF shows the design as it prints, with a made-up student. Save design keeps it (each save is kept under Versions, so an earlier one can be brought back). Report cards keep the standard design until you press Use this design; from then on every report card - the PDF and the page staff, parents and students see - is drawn from the saved design. Use the standard design goes back. Lock stops anyone changing it by accident.

Every tool in the studio, with pictures, is explained in {{ page('design-studios', 'Design studios') }}.

Troubleshooting: report cards

A student has no report card, or it says "Waiting for N results".
A card is ready only when every one of the student's results for the term is released. Release the ones still entered, verified or approved, and the card appears.
Attendance shows nothing, or too few days.
Attendance counts the days the class register was taken for that term. Days nobody marked are not counted; take the register daily under Attendance.
The position in class is missing, or seems wrong.
Position counts only students whose card is ready; equal scores share a place (1st, 2nd, 2nd, 4th). Once the rest of the class's results are released, it is right for everyone.
The class teacher's comment or signature is missing.
The comment is written on the Report cards page (Comments); the teacher draws or uploads their signature on My signature. The head's title, name and signature are on Report card settings.
Parents still see the standard report card.
Press Use this design in the report card studio, after saving. A change can take up to a minute to reach every report card.
A table looks cramped with many subjects.
Make the results table taller, or leave out a column (Assignment, Project, Remark) in its settings; the rows share the table's height.

Bringing in past results

Step 3 of Bulk import brings in a student's graded results from an earlier school year. It takes one row per student, subject, term and session. Required columns: admission_no, session, term, subject, exam_score, exam_max. Optional: test_score, test_max, notes. Leave the test columns empty when no test was recorded.

  • The session and the subject must already exist. Earlier sessions are created by step 2, and subjects are created under Subjects.
  • Results are released straight away, so they appear on the student's academic record and on their report cards. Each one is recorded as migrated, with the administrator who imported it.
  • A subject, term and session that already has a result for that student is left as it is and reported. Running a file twice never changes what is on record.
  • Graduates are included, so the results of students who have left are brought across.
  • It needs the permission to release results, or the bulk-import permission.
