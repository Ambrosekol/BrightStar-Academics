Everything in the School workspace — students, subjects, assignments, results, attendance, timetables — is organised around classes, sessions and terms.

Classes

Every school starts with the standard list already in place — Primary 1 to 6, JSS 1 to 3, SSS 1 to 3 — under Classes, so this is usually the one step already done on the setup checklist. You can rename, reorder or add to this list if your school uses different names.

Crèche and Nursery (Crèche, Nursery 1 to 3) are there too, switched off. A school that runs them switches them on with Activate; a school that is only primary, or only secondary, simply leaves the classes it does not run switched off. Crèche and Nursery pupils work on paper: they never sign in, and no online test, assignment or project can be set for them. Their teachers record their scores under Results & Records as usual, and their parents see the fees and the report card in the parent portal.

SSS departments. In SSS 1 to 3 every student belongs to a department: Science, Art or Commercial, chosen on the student's record. A subject can be taken by one, several or all departments (see Subjects below), and the portal keeps to that: a student only sees the tests of subjects their department takes, work issued to the whole class only goes to the students who take the subject, and a subject teacher can be limited to one department.

Sessions and terms

An academic session is a school year, e.g. "2025/2026" — set up and switched under Academic Sessions. One session is always the current one; new enrolments, results and attendance are filed against it. A term is First, Second or Third Term (or "Full Session" for an annual view) — terms are just a choice on a form, not something you create.

Subjects

Subjects shows every subject as a list, or as a grid of subjects against classes, so a class missing a subject stands out; the search narrows either at once. + New subject and Edit open a pop-up: the name and code, the classes that take it, and (for SSS) the departments that take it. When editing, the same pop-up lists each class with its lock, where you can lock or unlock it, and the School Admin can apply the final lock.

Subjects are not seeded: a brand-new school has none, which is the second step of the setup checklist. Creating a subject attaches it to one or more classes at the same time, and a subject you have added is then listed on every form that asks for a subject. A subject that is not yet attached to the class you pick on a form is still listed, marked as not yet offered to that class. Saving the work offers the subject to the class, so you never have to go back to Subjects first. A subject can be locked once its results are being finalised, which stops further changes to it for that class.

A subject also says which SSS departments take it: tick Science for Physics, Art and Commercial for Literature, and leave all unticked for a subject every department takes, such as English and Mathematics. Junior classes ignore this.

The assignment, project, timetable and result forms all follow this rule, so a subject you cannot find in a drop-down is almost always one that has not been created yet, not one that has been hidden.

Moving a class up a session

Promotion shows the four steps across the top (class progression, a draft, review and approval, commit) with the ones done ticked. Class progression opens a pop-up with every class and the class it moves up to; choosing saves at once. Make a draft from one session into the next, then open it (a pop-up): every student with the class they would move to and the decision for them. Choose a class with the chips and set one decision for everyone shown (a whole class repeating, say), then change any student on their own. A run is saved, approved, then committed; a committed or cancelled run stays readable.

At the end of a session, Promotion lets you generate a draft of who moves where, for every student currently enrolled — using progression rules you set once beforehand under Class progressions (e.g. "JSS 1 students go to JSS 2"), so the draft already guesses correctly for almost everyone.

Opening that draft shows one row per student, each with a decision you can change one at a time or in a batch: promote (the usual case), repeat the same class, transfer to a class other than the usual next one, graduate (leaving school entirely), hold (decide later) or withdraw. A draft can be saved and returned to as many times as you like — nothing moves yet. Only once you approve it, and then commit it, does every student's enrolment actually move into the new session; before that point, the whole exercise is completely reversible. The Promotion page itself keeps a table of every run ever generated, committed ones included, so you can always open one again later and see exactly what it did and to how many students.
