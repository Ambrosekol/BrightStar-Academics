A mark goes through the same journey everywhere it comes from — a test, an assignment, a manually entered score — before it ever reaches a student or parent.

Entered, verified, approved, released

Under Results & Records, a result moves through four stages: entered, verified, approved, released. Only released results count toward a student's official term result and their report card; the earlier stages let more than one person check a mark before it becomes visible to anyone outside staff. Opening a student's own term has one button that releases every subject of it at once, instead of one at a time; going the other way, Release the whole class's term at once (from the students list, before you pick one) previews exactly who is ready — how many subjects, how many already released, how many still waiting — before releasing everyone who actually has something approved, in one step.

How a term result is worked out

Each subject's term result is Continuous Assessment (test, assignment and project, out of 40 between them, in shares your school sets once) plus an exam out of 60. Work that has been removed stops counting; the arithmetic is worked out fresh every time it is needed, from the results themselves, so it can never disagree with what is actually on record.

Report cards

A student's report card for a term appears — to the student, a linked parent, and staff — the moment every one of that student's results for the term has been released, whether by hand or by a session's own release date. One result still waiting holds the whole card back. It shows the school's own branding, each subject's marks and grade, the overall percentage, the class average, and (once at least one has been rated) affective and psychomotor trait ratings, alongside the class teacher's comment and the head's signature.

Open Report cards from the navigation rail to see, for a class, session and term you choose, which students already have a ready card and which are still waiting (and why — which subject has not been released yet). From there you can open one student's card to read it exactly as they would see it, download it as a PDF, or — if every student in the class is ready — download the whole class's cards as one PDF at once, for printing or filing.

The School workspace's Overview page also shows, for the current term, the single highest-scoring student in each class you can see — a quick "who's leading" view, updated automatically as results are released, needing nothing set up.

A class teacher writes the comment and rates traits — Punctuality, Neatness, Leadership and the like — from the same page, and can do so before results are even released. Whoever last changes a comment or a rating is credited for it; saving the page unchanged never takes over someone else's.

When a card becomes ready, the parent is told automatically — an in-app alert, an email and a WhatsApp message — that it is ready to view and download. Nobody has to remember to announce it.

Setting up how a report card looks

Two small pages decide what appears at the bottom of every report card in the school: Report card settings sets the head of school's title (Principal, Headmistress, and so on) and their signature image, once for the whole school, plus how much of a subject's mark comes from Continuous Assessment versus the exam (see above). My signature is different: it is where any member of staff uploads their own signature image, for use wherever their own name appears signed on a document.

Bringing in past results

Step 3 of Bulk import brings in a student's graded results from an earlier school year. It takes one row per student, subject, term and session. Required columns: admission_no, session, term, subject, exam_score, exam_max. Optional: test_score, test_max, notes. Leave the test columns empty when no test was recorded.

  • The session and the subject must already exist. Earlier sessions are created by step 2, and subjects are created under Subjects.
  • Results are released straight away, so they appear on the student's academic record and on their report cards. Each one is recorded as migrated, with the administrator who imported it.
  • A subject, term and session that already has a result for that student is left as it is and reported. Running a file twice never changes what is on record.
  • Graduates are included, so the results of students who have left are brought across.
  • It needs the permission to release results, or the bulk-import permission.
