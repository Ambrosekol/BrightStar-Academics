A student account is never self-registered — staff always create it, one at a time or many at once — and it always starts on a one-time password the student must replace at first sign-in.

The students list

Students opens on every class at a glance: how many students each has, boys and girls, and how many can sign in to the student portal, with a count of anyone not placed in a class this session. Choose a class with the chips at the top (or a row of the table) and its register loads in place; the search box finds a student in that class, or in any class when none is chosen.

Clicking a student opens them in a pop-up over the register: their class, department and admission number at the top, four figures (term results, overall mark, assignments, projects), and one tab at a time for their Profile (personal details, parent or guardian, and their portal login), Term results, Every mark, Assignments, Projects and School journey. Edit record, Fee account, a new password, and Correct on a journey entry open in a second pop-up on top; saving there brings you back to the student, up to date. Close a pop-up with ×, Escape or a click outside it.

Registering one student

+ Register a student opens in a pop-up and creates the student record, enrols them in the class and session you choose, and generates their admission number and login in one step. The admission number is always generated from your school's own numbering rules — nobody types one in by hand, so it can never clash with another student's.

For a student in SSS 1 to 3, the form also asks for their department (Science, Art or Commercial). It can be left as Not chosen yet and filled in later; until then the student is treated as taking every subject of their class, so nothing is hidden from them.

Bringing in new students at once

Use Bulk import from the Students page to bring students in from a spreadsheet. The page has three steps, shown one at a time, and they are done in order: students, then their enrolment history, then their past results. Each step has its own template to download and its own rules.

Step 1 creates each student exactly as registering one by hand does. Every column is required, and every row must fill them all in: first_name, middle_name, last_name, gender, class, guardian_name, guardian_email and guardian_phone. The guardian's details drive the alerts parents receive and their sign-in, so a row without them is skipped. A row that is not valid is skipped and reported by its line number; every other row is still imported. Admission numbers are generated, never typed.

The generated usernames and one-time passwords for an import are shown once, on the results page, and can be downloaded from there as a CSV. They are never stored anywhere in readable form afterwards, so copy or download them before you navigate away.

A third way in: the entrance exam

A student can also arrive through the {{ page('admissions', 'admissions waitlist') }}, in the Entrance workspace: once a candidate has sat their entrance exam, an admissions officer can admit them straight into a real student record, the same way as the other two paths above.

Enrolment history: each student's past years

Step 2 brings in a student's past classes, one row per student per year, matched by admission number. Required columns: admission_no, level, session. Optional: enrolled_at, completed_at, outcome, notes.

  • Dates must be written YYYY-MM-DD, for example 2023-09-11, in both enrolled_at and completed_at. Leave completed_at empty while the year is open.
  • outcome is promoted, repeated, graduated or withdrawn. A graduated row archives the student once that year is recorded.
  • A session is matched by its name, such as 2023/2024. It may be a session the school has since archived.
  • If the session does not exist and it is an earlier year than the current session, the import creates it. The new session is inactive and is never made the current session, and the import records who created it and why. An import never changes which session is current.
  • A session that is later than the current one, or that is not written as 2023/2024, is refused. Create those by hand under Academic sessions.

Creating an earlier session, and archiving a graduate, need the permissions to import students and to deactivate students. The bulk-import permission covers both (see Roles, permissions and scope).

A student's account afterwards

From a student's pop-up you can edit their details, reset their password to a new one-time password (shown once, with a button to print it), or turn their sign-in off or on without touching anything else about their record. Enrolment history — previous schools, earlier levels — is kept separately and can be corrected with a reason if it was entered wrong.

Archiving a student who has left

When a student leaves, archive them rather than deleting them. Archiving keeps their whole record and takes them out of everything current:

  • they leave the class register and the student count;
  • their sign-in stops working straight away, including a session that is already open;
  • their results, report cards and fee history stay on file.

To archive one student, open them and use Archive this student at the foot of their Profile. To archive several, tick them in the class register; a bar appears with a box for the reason and Archive selected. You can give a reason, such as "Left the school" or "Transferred out". Archiving needs the permission to deactivate students, and it applies only to students in your own classes.

A graduate is archived automatically when a history import records their graduating year (see step 2 above).

Archived students are kept in Archived students, which only a School Admin can open. Each archived student opens in the same pop-up as everyone else, marked as archived, with their results and school journey. A School Admin can restore one from that pop-up, or tick several in the list and use Restore selected; it puts them back in their class register and turns their sign-in back on.

Parents keep access to an archived child. The child's record, results and fee account stay on the parent's portal, and only the child's own sign-in stops.
Deactivating a student hides them without marking them as having left. The deactivate switch does not work on an archived student: restore them first.
